# Report - Learning from Messy Human Labels (CIFAR-10N)

Q1. Noise rate is 40.2% (18109 of 45000 training labels). Three class pairs, automobile<->truck (2373), cat<->dog (1758), deer<->horse (916), cause 27.9% of all wrong labels. The biggest single directions are truck->automobile (1247), automobile->truck (1126), cat->dog (924). The noise is structured, not uniform: 72.0% of wrong labels stay within the same super-group (animal->animal or vehicle->vehicle).

Q2. Memorization starts around epoch 38. Training accuracy on the mislabeled subset (fit to the wrong label) bottoms out near 12.6% at epoch 18; from epoch 38 it stays 5+ points higher and climbs to 67.4% by epoch 60, while correct-subset accuracy reaches 97.3%. Clean-label validation accuracy peaks at epoch 45 (81.6%) and falls to 71.7% at the end. Noisy-label validation accuracy peaks at epoch 45, which we use for early stopping.

Q3. On our own clean-label check, the best single signal was DivideMix loss (AUROC 0.969); the submitted rank ensemble scores AUROC 0.972, precision@k 0.893. Failure mode: systematic human confusions. For 69.5% of missed wrong labels the held-out model itself predicts the wrong label, because the image genuinely looks like that class (most missed: cat->dog, truck->automobile), so its loss stays low.

Q4. Best was ELR+mixup (2-net ensemble): noisy-val 58.9% (clean-val 88.7%) vs CE 51.9% final / 56.2% early-stopped; others: divmix 57.6%, elr 58.5%. It helped, beating both final and early-stopped CE. ELR keeps predictions close to the model's own early (mostly correct) predictions, cancelling gradients from wrong labels; mixup blends images and labels, so no single wrong label can be memorized exactly; averaging two networks reduces variance.

Q5. Measured by clean-label validation accuracy, elrmix beats the CE baseline by +17.0 points on human noise and +17.3 on synthetic noise (CE baseline: 71.7% real, 75.3% synthetic). The ranking did not change and the gain is almost identical. The difference is in the baseline: CE suffers more from human noise. Random wrong labels contradict the image and are harder to fit, while human noise sits in look-alike pairs (cat/dog, car/truck), looks plausible and is learned like signal.
